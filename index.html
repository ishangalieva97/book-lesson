2 Pre-Teaching Vocabulary: Book Elements & The Illustrator's World

Hover over each card to focus. These are the key terms you'll need for today's lesson.

Cover /ˈkʌvə/

The front outer part of a book that protects the pages.

💡 "Can a cover be hard or soft?" — Yes, it can be either.

Spine /spaɪn/

The narrow side of a book where pages are glued; visible on a shelf.

💡 "Do you find the spine on the front or the side?" — On the side.

Dust jacket /ˈdʌst ˌdʒækɪt/

A removable printed paper cover that protects the hardcover.

💡 "Is it permanent or removable?" — Removable.

Title page /ˈtaɪtl peɪdʒ/

The first printed page inside with the full title and author's name.

💡 "Is this the same as the cover?" — No, it's inside the book.

Front matter /frʌnt ˈmætə/

Pages before the main story: title page, copyright, table of contents.

💡 "Do you read the story here?" — No, it's introductory material.

Typography /taɪˈpɒɡrəfi/

The style, arrangement, and appearance of printed text (fonts, spacing).

💡 "Can typography make a page feel scary or elegant?" — Absolutely!

Layout /ˈleɪaʊt/

The way text and images are arranged on a page.

💡 "Can a layout be clean or cluttered?" — Yes, both are possible.

Illustrator /ˈɪləstreɪtə/

A person who creates pictures and artwork for books.

💡 "Do they write the words or draw the pictures?" — Draw the pictures.

Brief /briːf/

Instructions from a publisher telling the illustrator what to create.

💡 "Does the brief give rules or total freedom?" — It gives rules and direction.

Palette /ˈpælɪt/

The range of colours an illustrator chooses for a specific project.

💡 "Can a palette be just black and white?" — Yes, or many colours.

3 Reading: A Children's Book Illustrator

Read the text carefully. Vocabulary words are highlighted.

I'm Sarah, and I bring stories to life. When I get a new brief from a publisher, I first read the manuscript carefully. Then, I start sketching the characters. I have to think about the entire book design, not just the pictures. The typography must match my drawing style. I plan the layout of each page: where the text goes and where the Goblin hides. I usually choose a warm colour palette because the story is cozy, but sometimes the author wants a scary scene on the title page! The hardest part is often the cover. It needs to look good even as a small thumbnail on a website, and you must remember to leave space for the text on the spine. Without a good dust jacket, a hardcover book looks naked.

📖 Comprehension Questions (use new vocabulary in your answers):

2. What does she plan to decide where text and images sit?

3. Why is designing the spine important?

4. What word describes her choice of 'warm colours'?

5 Freer Practice: Mini-Brief for a Designer

You are a writer hiring an illustrator for your new book. Work in groups and prepare a creative brief.

📋 Your Book: "The Last Robot in the Library" (or invent your own title!)

  • 🎨Cover: What must be on the cover? (Main character? Action scene?)
  • 🌈Colour Palette: What colours fit the mood? Why?
  • 🔤Typography: Old-fashioned, futuristic, messy, or elegant?
  • 📄Title Page: Describe an interesting detail for the layout.
  • 📚Spine: What information should appear there?
💬 Example starter: "For our cover, we need the main character — a robot — holding a dusty book. The palette should be sepia and dark blue because the library is old and mysterious. The typography should look like old metal stamps..."

🎤 Present your brief to the class using as many new words as possible!
